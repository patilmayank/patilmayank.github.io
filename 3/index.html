A.3: Warp the images (20 pts)

Include results of nearest-neighbor and bilinear interpolation. Show rectification examples.

For image rectification, Billinear Interpolation for inverse mapping results in a smoother warped image because it takes all neigboring cells into account with a weighted average. That's why the sidewalk sign looks smoother when it was warped with Billinear interpolation. On the other hand, nearest neighbor interpolation is quicker (but not asymptotically) because it just has to take the nearest pixel which eliminates the need for the weighting computation. Asymptotically both methods have the same runtime, but Billinear interpolation is about 1.5 times slower than nearest neighbor interpolation.

For image blending once I computed the warped image via the homography matrix given a set of correspondence points, I used a weighted average of the pixels from the left warped image and right image if they were in the overlapping region. For each pixel in the overlapping output blend, I computed the weighted horizontal distance from each image based on its distance from the image boundary, which was the right side of the warped left image, and the left boundary of the right image. Pixels closer to the right image's boundary would get more weight from the right image than the left warped image, and pixels closer to the warped left image's right boundary would get more weight from the left warped image than the right image. This approach helps visually eliminate the vertical edge boundary and produces a smoother transition between the images in the blended mosaic.
